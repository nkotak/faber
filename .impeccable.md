## Design Context

### Users
- **Primary**: Neal himself, at the desk, long review sessions (1-3 hours). Needs density, keyboard-first navigation, signal-to-noise ratio.
- **Secondary**: Neal on a laptop / couch for quick check-ins. Must stay fast and legible at small screens.
- **Tertiary**: Trusted collaborators (mentor, partner) who may be shown the dashboard. Implication: privacy-aware presentation — comp figures, personal notes, private projections should be easy to mask for a view-only shared session.

### Brand Personality
Three words: **operational, considered, unmistakable**.

Voice:
- Direct. Never chirpy, never apologetic, never corporate-chatty.
- The product is a *tool*. Tools earn respect by being sharp, not by explaining themselves.
- Copy leans terse — think log lines and trade-ticket labels, not onboarding prose.

Emotional goals:
- Confidence at a glance: "the system knows what it's doing."
- Focus: the interface disappears while you work, then becomes extraordinary when you notice.
- Trust: no gimmicks that obscure data; the data is the star.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Editorial-meets-terminal.** A magazine's sense of typography and margin discipline, crossed with a terminal's keyboard-first operational feel.

- **Typography duo**: A distinctive editorial serif (for headlines, report prose, section titles) paired with a considered monospace (for data columns, keybindings, filenames, scores). No Inter. No Fraunces. No IBM Plex. Look further.
- **Color**: Tinted neutrals. Brand hue bleeds subtly through every surface (chroma 0.005-0.01 on backgrounds). A single warm accent — the rarest color on the page — reserved for state changes and action affordances.
- **Density**: Information-dense but breathing. Long lines capped at ~72ch in prose surfaces. Data tables get fixed-width columns with clear optical rhythm.
- **Both themes, system-preference-respecting.** Tokens are theme-agnostic.

**Anti-references** (what this is NOT):
- Linear (too sterile, too saas-uniform).
- Notion (too cardy, too forgiving).
- Any "dashboard template" on ThemeForest.
- Glowing cyan-on-dark AI aesthetics.
- Rounded-corner card grids with pastel gradient avatars.

**References for mood** (not for copying):
- The New Yorker's column discipline (but with mono for data, not serif for everything).
- A Bloomberg terminal's operational feel (but quieter, with classical typography instead of phosphor UI).
- Working Not Working's editorial confidence (but denser, for a dashboard).
- The inside of a 1960s hardcover atlas — considered labels, precise grids, subtle but decisive color work.

### Ambition Level
**Go all-out on the overdrive moments.** User explicitly chose the maximum setting. Specifically:

- Row → detail View Transitions (shared-element morphing; this is the #1 moment).
- Virtual-scrolled apps table at 60fps for 1k+ rows with animated filter/sort transitions.
- Scroll-driven choreography in the report viewer (TOC that moves with reading position, marginalia that surfaces contextually).
- Theme switcher via View Transitions radial reveal from the clicked origin.
- Job chips with spring-physics entrance/exit and streaming progress.
- Keyboard command palette with live fuzzy search.

Reduced-motion opt-out replaces every cinematic moment with a tasteful static alternative.

### Design Principles
1. **Data is the protagonist.** Chrome is recessive; the table, the report, the score badge carry the emotion.
2. **Typography does the heavy lifting.** No icons propping up weak hierarchy. The serif-mono duo should be immediately memorable.
3. **Every interaction has a reason.** No splashy motion for motion's sake — motion reveals state, confirms action, or establishes spatial continuity.
4. **Keyboard is first-class.** Every button has a shortcut. The command palette is never more than a keystroke away. Mouse is supported, not privileged.
5. **Theme isn't an afterthought.** Both light and dark feel intentional — neither is a filter on the other. Light is warm and paper-like; dark is deep and architectural.
6. **Collaborator view respects privacy.** Comp/financial figures can be masked with a single toggle. No surprises when sharing a screen.
